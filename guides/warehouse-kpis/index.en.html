Warehouse KPIs: The 10 Most Important Metrics with Formulas

Running a warehouse takes a few – but the right – metrics. Here are the ten KPIs I use most often in practice, each with its formula, meaning and a tip from day-to-day warehouse work.

by Keywan Fakhri, Industrial Engineer (M.Sc.), Konstanz, Germany · Updated: 15 October 2026

  1. On-time delivery rate
  2. Perfect order rate
  3. Picking accuracy
  4. Picking productivity
  5. Inventory turnover
  6. Days of supply
  7. Inventory accuracy
  8. Order cycle time
  9. Storage utilization
  10. Cost per order

01On-time delivery rate

On-time orders ÷ all delivered orders × 100

Shows how reliably promised delivery dates are met. For customers often the single most important number.

✓ Measure against the promised date, not the requested date – otherwise you mix warehouse and sales problems.

02Perfect order rate

Error-free orders (on time, complete, undamaged, correctly documented) ÷ all orders

The strictest quality metric: an order only counts if everything is right.

✓ Record the error type as well – then you see which cause lowers the rate most.

03Picking accuracy

Correctly picked lines ÷ all picked lines

Measures the error rate in picking. Every error costs returns, re-deliveries and trust.

✓ Values well above 99 % are common in well-organized warehouses. Below that, check bin labelling and scanner use.

04Picking productivity

Picked lines ÷ labor hours

Productivity in picking – the basis for staff planning and cost comparison.

✓ Only compare similar order profiles: piece picking and pallet picking have completely different rates.

05Inventory turnover

Cost of goods used (year) ÷ average inventory

How often stock is renewed per year. Higher turnover ties up less capital.

✓ Calculate turnover per item group (ABC) – a single total hides slow movers.

06Days of supply

Current stock ÷ average daily consumption

How many days stock lasts at normal consumption.

✓ Combine days of supply with replenishment lead time to spot shortages before they happen.

07Inventory accuracy

Locations without discrepancy ÷ counted locations

How well system stock matches physical stock.

✓ Regular cycle counting keeps accuracy high without stopping the warehouse.

08Order cycle time

Shipping time − order receipt time

How quickly an order leaves the warehouse.

✓ Split the time into waiting, picking and packing – usually waiting is the largest share.

09Storage utilization

Occupied locations ÷ available locations

Shows how much capacity is in use.

✓ Above roughly 85–90 % utilization, performance often drops noticeably as relocations and search times increase.

10Cost per order

Warehouse costs (staff, space, material) ÷ number of orders

Links performance and cost – ideal for pricing talks and make-or-buy decisions.

✓ Use the same cost base every month, otherwise values are not comparable.

Which KPIs to start with?

Start with three or four KPIs you can measure reliably – usually on-time delivery, picking accuracy, picking productivity and cost per order. Only once these numbers are stable every month is it worth adding more.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between on-time delivery and perfect order rate?

On-time delivery usually measures punctuality only. The perfect order rate also checks quantity, quality and documents.

How many KPIs should a warehouse have?

Five to eight KPIs are enough for management. More numbers rarely lead to better decisions.

How often should warehouse KPIs be measured?

Operational KPIs daily or weekly, cost and inventory KPIs monthly.
